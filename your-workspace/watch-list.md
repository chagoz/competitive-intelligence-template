## Why watch lists matter

A watch list is not a news feed. It is a defined set of topics the system actively monitors every cycle. Without it, research scans broadly and misses depth. With it, every edition answers the same question: what moved on the things we actually care about?

## What makes a good topic

Each topic needs three things, and skipping the third is the most common failure mode:

1. **Why you watch it.** The actual stake, not just interest.
2. **What you scan for.** Specific, checkable signals, not a vague mandate.
3. **A stated noise floor.** The thing everyone in your category says or does that would look like a signal but isn't. Every real topic has one, and naming it up front is what stops a quiet vocabulary trend from being reported as a finding. "Everyone claims to be easy to use" is a noise floor. A claim with no observable change behind it scores low; a documented design change, a permission added, a default flipped, scores high.

**A topic can outlive the decision it was opened to inform.** If you open a topic to help decide something, and the decision gets made, don't delete the topic reflexively, ask whether it now serves a different purpose (tracking whether the market moved where you deliberately didn't, for instance) before retiring it.

**A topic watching design decisions, not just claims, is worth having if "being too capable" or "being too complex" is a real loss condition for you** (see the loss patterns section of `about-us.md`). Competitors rarely claim to be adding complexity, so a topic that scans for what's observable in the product itself, not what's said about it, can catch something no competitor would ever announce.

## For a forked version, choosing your topics

Ask:
1. What is the primary competitive battleground in your category right now?
2. What external forces (regulation, technology, market shifts) most affect your buyers?
3. Is there a decision your own company is about to make where the market's direction is a useful input? That's a good candidate topic, and it's fine for its purpose to change once the decision is made rather than for the topic to disappear.

Limit to five, and leave a seat open. Every topic needs its own noise floor stated, the count alone won't protect you from noise.
