## What each tier means, and why it exists

**Tier 1, direct competitors.** The ones who actually cost you deals. Gate-keeper rule: a company only belongs here if you've lost real deals to them, or prospects actively name them in evaluations. Not "similar," actually competing. Keep this list short, three to eight companies. Every one gets scanned every week, even when quiet, so a long Tier 1 list means wasted effort on companies that aren't actually costing you anything.

**Tier 2, adjacent.** Related problem, similar buyer, or visibly expanding toward your territory. Not yet costing you deals. Scanned only when genuinely eventful, not every cycle. Five to ten companies is typical.

**Tier 3, partnership or ecosystem lens.** Not a threat. Either influences your buyers from outside your category, or is a potential data or integration partner. Small list, two to five names.

**Tier 4, niche or emerging.** Specialist in one narrow dimension, or too early-stage to assess properly yet. Watched for one reason: escalation. If one of these gets named in a real deal, it moves to Tier 1.

**Tier 5, systemic alternatives.** Not a dedicated tool at all, the default your buyer would fall back on instead of buying anything (a spreadsheet, an existing enterprise system, doing it manually). Usually just one or two entries, but worth naming, this is often the real competitor for a slow-moving buyer.

## What to capture per company

*For each competitor you add, note enough to make the next reality check fast rather than a fresh investigation each time:*

- **Name and category**, what they actually sell, in your own words
- **Tier rationale**, one line, why this company sits at this tier specifically
- **AI-native flag**, if applicable, cross-cutting, a company can be AI-native and any tier
- **Escalation trigger** (Tier 2 and below only), the one line answering "what would cause this company to move to Tier 1"
