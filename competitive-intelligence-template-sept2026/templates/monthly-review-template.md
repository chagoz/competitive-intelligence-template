# Monthly Page Template

*One page per month, produced by the monthly reality check. Replaces the previous pair of sibling pages, a review and a working record, which carried the same month and date and were indistinguishable from outside.*

*Mirrored as a Notion page. Both copies must be updated together or they drift, which is what happened to the flash report template.*

*Who this is for: Product, Sales and C-level. A reading document, not a research archive. Voice rules live in `language-guidelines.md` Part 3: observational, impact first, proof at the close, altitude rule, data-sufficiency gate.*

Sections 4 and 7 are new as of September 2026. Everything else is the shape the August 2026 edition already used and which worked.

**Two heading levels maximum.** This is decision support, not reference documentation. Every heading lands in the table of contents, and more levels make it unreadable. Structure below H2 uses dividers and bold paragraph titles.

## Section 2, the three things

Exactly three, confirmed at the editorial gate. Not two, not four. Each is a bold lead sentence carrying the finding, then one or two sentences of explanation. Numbered.

The grey subtitle states that these were confirmed at the gate and on what date. That is the trust signal for the whole page.

---

## Section 3, key learnings

**Headers are the finding, not a standing name.** "The bundle that beats us is now private-equity funded", not "Competitor activity". Impact first, per Part 3. This is why the section names change every month and should.

**Header emojis come from the Part 1 table.** Never an inline marker. The threat circles mean a score and must not be borrowed as section symbols; a reader who sees one in a heading reads the whole section as scored.

**Shape of each learning.**
Bold thesis line, then prose. Then the source block: quote block, grey italic, carrying both axes with their markers, the origin chip, the link, the date and the certainty chip. One source line per source, so a reader can check each claim separately.

**Yellow highlight** on the single sentence that carries the surprise. One per learning at most, often none.

**How many.** However many were confirmed at the gate, usually two or three. A key learning that cannot carry its own section header is not a key learning.

## Section 4, the market lens

*New. Full rule: `analysis-rules.md` §2.6.*

**What it holds.** Three to five observations about the category rather than about a company, drawn from the Theme Watch activity matrix in the working record below.

**Where the observations come from.** Four inputs the cycle already collected: the platforms the category sits on, regulatory movement itself, unattributed losses read as buyer behaviour, and companies named by prospects that we do not track.

**Labelling.** An observation from the not-ours block of the matrix says so in the sentence. Never presented as one of our theme watches. The reader must be able to tell the difference between the market moving and us moving.

**Why it sits here.** After the company learnings and before the opportunities, because an opportunity should be able to draw on both.

**If nothing moved.** One grey line saying so. Do not pad it to reach three. A genuinely quiet category is a finding.

---

## Section 5, where the opportunity sits

Numbered, divider between each item. Observation plus why it matters, never an instruction. No imperative verb opening an item.

Each opportunity carries its proof, in the same source-block form as a key learning, where the proof is external.

Three is a comfortable maximum. This section is what the scanning reader is hunting for, so it needs to stay findable rather than becoming a list.

---

## Section 6, data-sufficiency note

Grey italic. States how many reality checks exist, and therefore whether the page is reporting current state or direction of travel. Two points are not a trend.

Also records any tier change made at this gate, with a pointer to where the reasoning lives.

---

## Section 7, the working record

Toggle, titled `Working record, [month]`. Holds the full research pass:

- The lost-deal table for the period, with the unattributed rows kept and read rather than discarded
- The Theme Watch activity matrix
- The key learning candidates that were not selected, with the reasoning both for and against each, so the cut stays visible
- Follow-ups closed this cycle
- Registry and record corrections made
- Date traps found
- Coverage gaps, stated honestly, with what could not be reached and why

**Never linked as a read.** It is the audit trail. The review above is the document.

**Why it lives here rather than as its own page.** Two sibling pages with the same month and date are indistinguishable from outside, and readers stopped being able to tell which one to open.

**Open question, September 4, 2026.** Whether the Theme Watch matrix belongs outside the toggle. The market lens is already the matrix's read, in the open, so the question is whether a reader needs the grid itself or only the observations drawn from it. Unresolved, revisit at the next template review.

## The Theme Watch activity matrix

Inside the working record toggle. The system's only market-level object: indexed by theme, not by company, read across every tracked company.

| Theme watch | Activity this cycle | Since last cycle | Reading |

**Rows, block one.** One per theme watch, in the order `about-us.md` lists them. That list is owned there and referenced here, never restated. It changes only when the company's strategy changes.

**Rows, block two.** Observed in the market, not ours. One row per observation. Open, rewritten each cycle, never merged into block one. A market theme does not become one of ours by appearing often enough; that is a strategy decision made by a human.

**Activity this cycle.** Dated evidence only, per `analysis-rules.md` §2.1. Company-attributed where a company did it, source-attributed where the input came from regulation, a loss or a platform. "Nothing this cycle" is written as such, not left blank.

**Since last cycle.** Heating, cooling or unchanged, read off the previous edition. Nothing new is stored to produce it.

**Reading.** One sentence with its threat marker. This is the scannable column.

**No trailing summary.** The matrix ends on its last row.

## Distribution

The review gets a Slack post, using the digest's mechanism: three drafted options, the channel recorded, an impact tracker row, reactions at 24 hours and one week.

A monthly document with no distribution does not get read, which is why this one went unread for two cycles before anyone noticed. Channel choice is editorial and varies by edition, so the tracker row records where it actually went.
