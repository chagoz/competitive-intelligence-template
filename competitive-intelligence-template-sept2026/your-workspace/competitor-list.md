## What each tier means

**Tier 1, direct competitors.** The ones actually costing you deals. Gate-keeper rule: you've lost real deals to them, or they're demonstrably named in an active evaluation, on a shortlist, quoted alongside you, or recorded in a deal as a named alternative. **A single mention in passing is not that.** A company named once is a profile, not a tier, it moves to Tier 1 on a second independent mention or a loss, not on arrival. Keep this list short, three to eight companies.

**Tier 2, adjacent.** Related problem or similar buyer, not yet costing you deals. Watch for whether the barrier between you is a real product gap or just a buyer-shape mismatch, the second is weaker than it looks and worth its own line in each cycle's review.

**Tier 3, partnership or ecosystem lens.** Not a threat. Influences your buyers from outside, or a potential data or integration partner.

**Tier 4, niche or emerging.** Specialist in one dimension, or too early to assess properly. Watched for one reason: escalation, a second independent mention or a lost deal moves it up.

**Tier 5, systemic alternatives.** Not a dedicated competitor at all, the platform or default your category sits on or gets absorbed into (an ERP, a spreadsheet). Read into the market lens, never scored on the threat axis, a platform isn't making a move against you, it's the ground competitors build on.

**AI-native flag, cross-cutting.** A company can be AI-native and any tier.

---

## What to capture per company

- **Name and category**, what they actually sell, in your own words
- **Tier rationale**, one line, specific to why this company sits at this tier
- **Escalation trigger** (Tier 2 and below), the line answering what would move it up
- As evidence accumulates, note things like a name variant you've seen it under, or a correction if you find you'd tracked something in error, both keep the list honest over time
